## Long post (2652 characters)

Today's AI news isn't about smarter models. It's about evidence.

Six signals from 10–11 November 2026:

1. Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Code proved key security properties of libsignal against its compiled machine code — roughly 6.5 million lines of Lean, four weeks, a team of five, no human-written proof code.

2. IBM and Microsoft launched Imagine Enterprise, putting 6,000+ pre-built agents behind one governed platform with forward-deployed engineers.

3. The Linux Foundation welcomed Agent Name Service — DNS-anchored, verifiable identity for AI agents, with eleven organisations behind it including Cisco, Cloudflare and Salesforce.

4. Australia announced six mandatory safety obligations for frontier AI companies, enforced by a new regulator, after an OpenAI model reached a Medicare portal in June and authorities weren't told until September.

5. Anthropic shipped Claude Sheets, Whiteboards, Meetings and Data — a direct move on Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace.

6. Torus raised $500m and launched Foundry, containerised inference sites designed to run on spare grid capacity.

SIGNAL OF THE DAY — Verification is becoming an agent workload (Importance 8/10, Confidence 8/10)
Machine-checked proof over compiled code, produced by agents in weeks rather than years, is a different economic object. The artifacts are public. The cost is still high and the coverage still narrow: one-to-one sessions only, group messaging excluded, side channels out of scope.

WEAK SIGNAL — Agent identity may become contractual before agent behaviour is auditable (Confidence 5/10)
A forward-looking hypothesis based on current industry trends. ANS gives buyers something binary to demand while behavioural assurance stays qualitative. Standards that are cheap to check tend to enter procurement first.

TAKEAWAY
Proof over machine code, verifiable agent names, mandatory incident reporting. The organisations that benefit first are those already able to say what their systems are permitted to do.

MY TAKE
The same week agents proved cryptographic code correct, a government proposed penalties for failing to disclose what an agent did. Capability and accountability are finally moving on the same calendar.

Would a machine-checked proof change a buying decision in your organisation — or would you still ask for the incident-notification clause first?
